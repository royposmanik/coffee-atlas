LOT 01 · GROWTH

From 93 to 177 million bags.

World consumption has grown by roughly 2% a year for three decades, and has not fallen in any year shown. Production swings with Brazil's harvest cycle and weather, so supply and demand trade places year to year.

LOT 02 · ORIGIN

Grown in the tropics, in few hands.

Coffee grows in a belt between the Tropics of Cancer and Capricorn. Brazil and Vietnam together supply more than half the world's beans; around 12.5 million farms grow the rest, most of them smallholdings.

Arabica grows at altitude and dominates speciality coffee. Robusta tolerates heat and pests, carries more caffeine, and fills most instant coffee. Vietnam grows almost only robusta.

LOT 04 · FLOW

Most of the harvest never reaches the cup.

Follow the mass. It takes about 5.5 kg of ripe cherries to make 1 kg of green coffee, and only about a fifth of the roasted coffee dissolves into the drink. Everything shown in stripes leaves the chain as a residue.

Carried forwardResidue or loss

LOT 05 · WASTE

What coffee leaves behind.

Coffee waste has two very different ends: organic residues produced in huge volumes near farms and in cities, and packaging that is small per cup but hard to recycle.

Modelled~6 Mt

Spent coffee grounds

Dry grounds left after brewing, every year. Most go to landfill, where they release methane. They can become compost, biofuel pellets or oil feedstock.

Mussatto et al. 2011, widely cited
Derived~48 Mt

Pulp, husk and mucilage

Fresh cherry residue at origin. Pulp dumped near rivers can deoxygenate water. It can be composted or sold as cascara tea.

LOT 06 · CLIMATE

Rising demand, shrinking land.

Demand keeps growing while warming pushes arabica uphill. The land that suits coffee today will not be the land that suits it in 2050.

~50%

of land now suited to coffee could become unsuitable by 2050 under a high-emission scenario (Bunn et al., 2015).

12.5 M

farms grow coffee worldwide, around 95% of them smaller than 5 hectares (Fairtrade / ICO estimates).

LOT 07 · SOURCES

Where the numbers come from.

Every figure is labelled reported, modelled or derived. Values are rounded; check the original publication before quoting them.

PrimaryInternational Coffee Organization (ICO)

Coffee Market Reports and historical statistics. Powers consumption, production, species split and regional shares.

SupplementalUSDA FAS · Coffee: World Markets and Trade

Twice-yearly supply and demand estimates by country. Useful cross-check for ICO production figures.

EnvironmentalPoore & Nemecek (2018), Our World in Data

EnvironmentalWater Footprint Network
